The_Vikings_in_Europe
[blockend:0_überschrift]	

[blockstart:0_text_0]
From the 8th until the 11th centuries, the Vikings held the world breathless. 
Searching for new areas for settlements, adventures and treasures, they left their homes as warriors and discoverers. They fell pillaging into the whole of Europe from Norway, Sweden and Denmark.
\n
[blockend:0_text_0]	

[blockstart:0_text_1]
Their dragonships seemed to appear from out of nowhere on the distant shores, they plundered the cities and vanished as suddenly as they had come. 
\n
\n
But the Vikings were far more than simply "wild barbarians" from the North. They were clever merchants, skilful seafarers, excellent craftsmen and shipbuilders. They traded goods all the way to Baghdad and even came as far as China on the Silk Route. 
[blockend:0_text_1]	




[blockstart:1_überschrift]
The_Settlement_of_Greenland
[blockend:1_überschrift]	
                                                                                                          
[blockstart:1_text_0]
The Danes and Norwegians had been spreading westwards since the late 8th century: Iceland was discovered in the year 870, Greenland in 985. This way, Leif Erikson was probably the first European to enter the American continent (about 1000). 
\n
\n
The Vikings were the most skilful shipbuilders and seafarers of their time. They also used longboats called "dragonships" for their raids. 
\n
[blockend:1_text_0]	

[blockstart:1_text_1]
For several hundred years, the Vikings lived between the Old and the New World and set out from there on their advantageous quests, both into the unknown West and into Europe. These quests were the subject of the sagas and ballads told and sung to the old at the campfire in the winter. One of the best-known myths was the saga of the fight of the gods against Evil - RAGNAROK.
[blockend:1_text_1]	


[blockstart:2_überschrift]
The_Normans_in_France
[blockend:2_überschrift]

[blockstart:2_text_0]
Mainly the Vikings who settled along the north-western coast of France in the middle of the 9th century were called "Normans". 
\n
\n
After the Vikings had spent decades invading France over and over again, and had even taken Paris several times, Charles The Simple, King of the Western Franks, gave one of their leaders by the name of Rollo the duchy of Normandy, a region at the lower Seine, in the year 911. \n
[blockend:2_text_0]

[blockstart:2_text_1]
In return, Rollo accepted the King as his feudal lord, converted to Christianity and protected France against further raids by his own countrymen. 
\n
\n
After only three generations, Normandy had become the most powerful French duchy. One reason for this development was the Normans' ability to adapt: They used the existing facilities and systems for themselves, extended them with a few changes and improved them.
[blockend:2_text_1]


[blockstart:3_überschrift]
Raids_in_England 
[blockend:3_überschrift]

[blockstart:3_text_0]
The raid on the monastery of Lindisfarne at the east coast of England in 793 was the first proven attack of the Vikings against Europe. This news spread like wildfire all over the Christian world and terrified everybody. 
\n
[blockend:3_text_0]

[blockstart:3_text_1]
But the attack on Lindisfarne was just a prelude. The Danish Vikings raided the English coasts during the next centuries, but invaded the interior of the country as well and occupied wide areas of England in the course of time.
A whole region further north was given the name Danelaw.
\n
\n
Around 990 England became part of the kingdom of Denmark.
In 1066 AD, a huge invasion army from Normandy crossed the Channel in dragonships. William the Conqueror beat the Anglo-Saxons in the Battle of Hastings on 14 October 1066. From then on, the Normans controlled the whole island.
[blockend:3_text_1]


[blockstart:4_überschrift]
The_Normans_in_Sicily 
[blockend:4_überschrift]

[blockstart:4_text_0]
While England became used to the new rulers, other Normans founded new principalities in Southern Europe. Far from their homes, they settled in wide parts of Southern Italy from 1017-1061 and sailed across the Mediterranean, which was a stronghold of Muslim pirates at that time. 
\n
\n
The Vikings and Normans felt particularly attracted by rich Sicily, which had already been ruled by the Saracens for more than 200 years. The first Vikings appeared there as mercenaries as early as 1017, and during constant skirmishes between 1060 and 1091, the Normans managed to snatch the whole island from the Saracens. 
\n
[blockend:4_text_0]

[blockstart:4_text_1]
At first, the Greek and Byzantine natives celebrated them as their liberators from the Muslim yoke. The Norman kingdom of Sicily was founded, a flourishing, well-ruled country, where for the first and only time in history, the three great Mediterranean cultures, i.e. the Western, Byzantine and Arab cultures, co-existed in relative harmony.
[blockend:4_text_1]


[blockstart:5_überschrift]
The_Arrival_in_Miklagard
[blockend:5_überschrift]

[blockstart:5_text_0]
Numerous Scandinavian sagas contain the name "Miklagard", which means "big town". This refers to Byzantium (Constantinople), the capital of the East Roman Empire, an incredibly important centre of power and trade. 
\n
\n
The Vikings were confronted with an absolutely strange, splendid and - in contrast to their own - highly developed civilisation when they reached Byzantium on their extensive trade journeys. 
\n
[blockend:5_text_0]

[blockstart:5_text_1]
There are two contracts known from that time which governed the rights and duties of the Scandinavian merchants in Byzantium. Since these merchants were actually needed in the city, they received numerous privileges such as baths and food free of charge. 
\n
\n
The Vikings had to enter Byzantium without weapons though, and they were only allowed to live in a certain district and had to register with the town council.
\n
\n
Today, numerous Byzantine coins and rune stones found in Scandinavia still refer to the journeys to Miklagard. Nowadays, Byzantium is part of Turkey and is known as Istanbul.
[blockend:5_text_1]


[blockstart:6_überschrift]
The_Waräger_Guard
[blockend:6_überschrift]

[blockstart:6_text_0]
Money could not only be made through trading in Byzantium: The Emperor constantly needed soldiers, and the Scandinavians, who were well used to fighting, were the right people for him. 
\n
\n
Many famous Vikings served as mercenaries in the so-called "Waräger Guard", among others Harald Hardradi (1015-1066 AD), who gained renown as the commander of the Emperor's bodyguard, and later became King of Norway. He fell in the fight against England. 
\n
[blockend:6_text_0]

[blockstart:6_text_1]
Above all, the warriors of the Guard were famous for their huge battleaxes, whose sight terrified every enemy, their bravery and absolute loyalty towards their commander and the Emperor (and of course their ability to hold their liquor). 
\n
\n
The Guard was also employed at numerous ceremonies and other official occasions, e.g. for weddings or when the Emperor's family went to church. This shows how well-respected the Scandinavians were by the Byzantine Emperors. 
[blockend:6_text_1]


[blockstart:7_überschrift]
Byzantine_Intrigues 
[blockend:7_überschrift] 

[blockstart:7_text_0]
Byzantium was an old, centralist monarchy of civil servants. Corruption and internal fights for power formed the daily routine of the Empire. 
\n
\n
Aristocrats and courtiers haggled about ranks and privileges and tried to reach the throne through perjuries and intrigues. The Waräger Guard often played a decisive part: The side they took could influence the result of many attempted coups.
\n
[blockend:7_text_0]

[blockstart:7_text_1]
Harald Hardradi of Norway was a famous general of the Waräger Guard. He conducted raids on Sicily, Italy, Bulgaria and England for years. According to legend, Harald was said to have an affair with the Empress, which caused serious conflicts in Byzantine politics.
[blockend:7_text_1]


[blockstart:8_überschrift]
Arabian_Scholars
[blockend:8_überschrift]

[blockstart:8_text_0]
In contrast to the more or less uncivilised peoples of Western and Central Europe, the Arabs were a highly developed civilisation. Around the year 1000, their powerful empire comprised the area from Spain over North Africa up to India. 
\n
\n
They respected the cultures of foreign peoples and were eager to learn and use their scientific findings. Thus they obtained much knowledge from the Greeks, especially in the field of mathematics. 
\n
[blockend:8_text_0]

[blockstart:8_text_1]
The university of Alexandria in Egypt played an important part in this context, and many Greek works were translated into Arabic. In this roundabout way, the Greek culture returned to Western Europe. 
\n
\n
While the majority of the population in Europe were analphabetic, famous doctors, inventors, geographers and other scientists were educated at Arabian universities. Literature and poetry were important for the Arabs as well, and the people loved fairytales. 
\n
\n
The greater part of the famous collection of tales "Arabian Nights" came from Arabia. 
[blockend:8_text_1]


[blockstart:10_überschrift]
The_Journey_to_Sarkland
[blockend:10_überschrift]

[blockstart:10_text_0]
The East was attractive to the Vikings, especially because of trade. They sailed the large Russian rivers and did not only make it to Byzantium, but also to the great Arabian markets at the Caspian Sea and even to Baghdad, the headquarters of the Abbaside caliphate. 
\n
\n
The legendary rich Caliphs of Baghdad made their city one of the most flourishing metropolises of the time and the cultural centre of the Arab world. They also supplied the foundation for many of the greatest fairytales.
\n
[blockend:10_text_0]

[blockstart:10_text_1]
The Vikings exploited Arabian silver mines; silver became their most important precious metal. They "bought" silver for similar goods, for example in Byzantium (fur, slaves, falcons, honey, wax, walrus tusks and strong swords made from steel).
\n
\n
The Abbaside caliphate, the area to the south of the Caspian Sea, was also called Sarkland by the Vikings.
[blockend:10_text_1]


[blockstart:11_überschrift]
Ragnarok_and_the_Midgard_Serpent
[blockend:11_überschrift]

[blockstart:11_text_0]
The old Nordic myth of Ragnarok can be found in the Germanic Völuspa, the introductory song of the Edda. It describes Ragnarok as the outbreak of the destructive and dark powers. 
\n
\n
The gods fall in this fight: The wolf Fenrir devours Odin, Thor kills the Midgard snake but dies through her poisonous breath on Wigrid's wide plains, 
\n
the giant Surtur kills Freyr, Midgard breaks apart in storm tides and blazes, stars fall into the sea. The sun goes black, the Earth sinks back to its original state of chaos.
\n
\n
But out of this, a new cosmos is created, and this world is pure and clean, bright and fertile, the world of a new beginning. It comprises the seeds of Evil as well, though - a new cycle begins.
\n
[blockend:11_text_0]

[blockstart:11_text_1]
In Nordic mythology, the Midgard serpent (Jormungand) is the sign of constant repetition and return. It is a giant demon snake and a symbol of the world's seas embracing the land. When she rolls in the water, her movements cause powerful storm tides.
\n
\n
After christianisation, Migdardsomr was identified with the Jewish Leviathan.
[blockend:11_text_1]
